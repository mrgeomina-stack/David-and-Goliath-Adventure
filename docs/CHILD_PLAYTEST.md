# Supervised child playtest — version 1.4

This is a session guide and blank observation sheet. No sessions with children have been conducted by the development agent, and no observations below are fabricated. Automated checks do not establish how children understand the game.

Use a small supervised group covering ages 5–8 and the actual classroom tablet or computer. Obtain the permissions your setting requires. Do not record names or other identifying information in this worksheet. Let each child control the game; avoid explaining the solution until they ask for help. Use captions, optional installed local narration, and the desired Arabic/English mode.

## First try: watch before teaching

- Can the child find Start and recognize David within 10 seconds?
- Can they explain the colourful instruction card in their own words?
- Do they discover movement and pick up a nearby sheep without adult hand positioning?
- Do they recognize the held sheep, the pen entrance, and the four-sheep progress display?
- Do they understand the 30-second timer and its amber/red warnings?
- Does a lion catch feel like a manageable retry? Stop or switch to Free Play if it causes distress.
- Can they identify the next chapter and an earned keepsake in the adventure book?

## Across the remaining chapters

Watch for misleading scenery in the maze, difficulty dragging armour to body parts, failure to recognize a smooth stone, or difficulty understanding hold/release in the sling game. Ask the child what they expect to happen before an action. Check that the armour collapse reads as playful and the food delivery feels like a reward.

Keep observations separate from automated QA. Compare results across at least two children per age band before describing the difficulty as calibrated. The 30-second limit is the product owner's explicit choice; if it proves too tight, present that evidence before changing it. Free Play remains available for unhurried classroom turns.

## Current automated coverage

Actual input events test four physical carries, emulated tablet pickup, timer pause/warnings/catch/retry, connected mazes, anatomical armour drops, unique stone collection, real projectile hits/misses, the complete narrative, map navigation, reward persistence, keyboard focus, colour-card bounds, and offline operation. Narration uses installed local voices only; an absent voice leaves captions available and displays a clear notice. Human voice quality, Arabic language review, classroom readability and child comprehension require the supervised session above.
